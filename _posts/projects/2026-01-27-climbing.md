Everyone seems to be climbing nowadays and the popularity of sports climbing has sky-rocketed over the last few years. 
It was even featured as a sport for the first time during summer Olympics in 2020.
But how did it all start?

Competition climbing nowadays consists of 3 distinct disciplines - lead climbing, bouldering and speed climbing.
The roots of climbing can be traced to late 19-th century Europe, where it started as an outdoor recreational activity.
Beginning of bouldering is linked to Fontainbleau and lead climbing to Dolomites, while speed climbing originated in Russia.
The first climbing competition ever organized was in 1985 at Sportroccia in Italy.
It was organized on the Parete dei Militi rock face in Valle Stretta, near Bardonecchia on the Italian–French border.
In 1986 first Competition climbing series was organized in Europe, mainly on outdoor rock.

In 1988, the Internation Climbing and Mounaineering Federation (UIAA) incorporated the rules began organizing indoor competitions.
In 1989, the first ever UIAA World Cup was held in over 7 locations, mostly in EU. First one was in Leeds.
The competitions were organized on outdoor walls in those years.
In 1990, the decision was made to only organize climbing competitions on artificial walls, as they provide more stable environments and also eliminate environmental impact.
Nine year later in 1998 bouldering and speed climbing were added, also first Youth Competitions
youth competition -> sport is more recognized.

Since then, the number of climbing competitions increases each year.
